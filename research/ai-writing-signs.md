# AI Writing Signs to Avoid (from Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing)

## Key Patterns to Never Use

### Words/Phrases to Avoid
- Em dashes (—) used in place of commas, colons, or parentheses
- "serves as" / "stands as" / "marks" / "represents"
- "boasts" / "features" / "maintains" / "offers"
- "vibrant" / "rich" / "profound" / "enhancing" / "showcasing"
- "exemplifies" / "commitment to" / "nestled" / "in the heart of"
- "groundbreaking" / "renowned" / "diverse array"
- "highlighting" / "underscoring" / "emphasizing"
- "ensuring" / "reflecting" / "symbolizing" / "contributing to"
- "cultivating" / "fostering" / "encompassing"
- "valuable insights" / "align with" / "resonate with"
- "pivotal" / "crucial" / "key role" / "key moment"
- "evolving landscape" / "focal point" / "indelible mark"
- "deeply rooted" / "setting the stage for"
- "I hope this helps" / "Certainly!" / "Of course!"
- "Not just X, but also Y" parallelisms
- "Despite challenges..." formula
- "Future Outlook" / "Future Prospects"

### Structural Patterns to Avoid
- Rule of three (adjective, adjective, adjective)
- Overuse of boldface for emphasis
- Em dashes used formulaically to punch up clauses
- Negative parallelisms ("not X, but Y")
- Superficial analyses attached with "-ing" phrases
- Vague attributions ("experts argue", "observers note")
- Elegant variation (avoiding repeating the same word by using fancy synonyms)
- Curly quotation marks
- Title Case In Headings Where Not Needed
- Canned emphasis on significance/legacy/broader trends

### What Human Writing Looks Like
- Uses "is" and "are" naturally (LLMs avoid copulatives)
- Shorter, punchier sentences mixed with longer ones
- Specific facts over generic praise
- Commas and parentheses instead of em dashes
- Repeats words when natural rather than forcing synonyms
- Conversational tone without being sycophantic
- Makes claims without over-explaining their significance

## Rules for DentaSmart Copy
1. Never use em dashes. Use commas, periods, or parentheses instead.
2. Write like you're explaining to a friend, not selling to a prospect.
3. Be specific: "$19.99 one-time" not "affordable pricing solutions"
4. Avoid triple-adjective lists.
5. Don't explain why something matters after stating it. Let the reader decide.
6. Use "is" and "has" instead of "serves as" or "features".
7. Keep sentences varied in length. Short ones hit harder.
8. No "not just X, but Y" constructions.
9. Avoid words: leverage, empower, seamless, comprehensive, robust, cutting-edge, holistic, innovative.
10. Write copy that could have been written by a dental hygienist texting a patient, not a marketing agency.
